**[4] Usability Test — Compass AI Practice Setup Flow** · `raw:2026-02-03-usability-test-compass-ai-practice-setup-flow`

> Objective
> The 2026-01-20 dashboard review found setups of Compass AI for a practice stopping most at Connect calendar (7 of 24 setups started) and then at Invite your team (3 of 24), and said a dashboard can't show why ( 2026-01-20-dashboard-review-compass-ai-practice-setup-completion/session-notes.md:45-46,72 ). This session watched administrators go through the practice setup flow to find out what happens at those two steps. It is about admin setup of Compass AI, not staff training.
> Method
> Method: Moderated usability test, remote, think-aloud, 60 min/session, one participant per session
> Researcher: Priya Patel (moderator)
> Full participant roster: see participants.md in this folder
> Participants: 6 — 4 Practice Administrators (P154-P157) and 2 IT Administrators (P158, P159)
> Steps tested, in order: 1 Sign in with Meridian SSO; 2 Practice details; 3 Connect calendar (read access to the practice's appointment schedule); 4 Invite your team (gives clinicians and staff access to Compass AI and sends them an email); 5 Review & finish.
> Build: Steps 3 and 4 are required in this build. There is no "Connect later" and no "Skip for now"; the only way past each step is to complete it.
> Design context: At session setup, the designer, Sam Okafor, stated that the calendar step is required because the scribe attaches recordings to scheduled encounters.
> Test environment: Participants signed in with their own Meridian SSO and were asked to set up as they would for their own practice. The environment was a test one: step 3 connected to a prepared sandbox calendar standing in for the practice's schedule, and step 4 used a prepared test staff list, so no real schedule was read and no real staff were emailed.
> Test accommodation: When a participant stopped at step 3, the moderator told them it was the sandbox calendar and let them continue. Every participant completed step 3 this way or on their own, so completing step 3 in this session is not a result.
> Not covered: The step 4 screen doesn't say when the invite email is sent, and these notes don't record when this build sends it. How Compass AI access is provisioned (through SSO groups or through these invites) wasn't known to the moderator and isn't recorded in the corpus.
> Key Findings
> [HIGH] (calendar: who can approve) 3 of 4 Practice Administrators (P154, P155, P157) stopped at Connect calendar and wouldn't connect it: it gives Compass AI access to patient appointments, and they did not think they could approve that. Each said it was IT's decision. The fourth (P156) asked the moderator whether connecting the schedule was theirs to approve before going on. All 4 continued only on the sandbox calendar (see Test accommodation).
> [MEDIUM] (calendar: what is read) Both IT Administrators (P158, P159) connected the calendar, but only after asking which fields are read (for example, whether visit reasons are included). The screen doesn't list them; the moderator couldn't answer, and both went on without an answer.
> [HIGH] (invite: timing and training) 3 of 4 Practice Administrators (P154, P156, P157) hesitated at Invite your team. They weren't sure whether the email goes out when they finish the step or at Review & finish, and they didn't want staff emailed before champion training is booked for the practice. P155 entered the test staff list and went on without comment.
> [MEDIUM] (invite: provisioning) One IT Administrator (P159) asked whether the invites create accounts or rely on the SSO groups staff are already in. The moderator couldn't answer, and the corpus doesn't record how access is provisioned.
> [LOW] (sign in and practice details) No issues at Sign in or Practice details: 6 of 6 participants completed both without help or comment on a problem.
> Representative Quotes
> "That's patient names and visit reasons. That's not my call — that's IT's." — Practice Administrator, P154
> "Is this mine to say yes to? I'd normally send anything like this to IT." — Practice Administrator, P156
> "If forty people get an email before anyone's booked training, my phone rings all afternoon." — Practice Administrator, P157
> "Before I connect it I want to know what it pulls. Times and providers, fine. Visit reasons, that's a different conversation." — IT Administrator, P158
> "Does this make them an account, or does it just use the groups they're already in? Because if it's a separate list, I'm the one keeping it in sync." — IT Administrator, P159
> Recommendations
> List the fields read from the appointment schedule on the Connect calendar step.
> Add a "Send to IT for approval" path on Connect calendar for an administrator who can't approve the access themselves.
> Say on the Invite your team screen when the invite email is sent.
> Let administrators schedule the invite email for a date, so it can go out once training is booked.
> Follow-ups / Open Questions
> How is Compass AI access provisioned: through SSO groups, through these invites, or both? Ask the setup flow's engineering team.
> When does this build send the invite email: at step 4 or at Review & finish?
> Check the setup dashboard after any copy change to these steps, to see whether setups still stop at Connect calendar and Invite your team.

**[6] Interview — IT Administrator on Schedule Access and Staff Invites** · `raw:2026-02-24-interview-it-administrator-schedule-access-and-staff-invites`

> Objective
> In the 2026-02-03 usability test, 3 of 4 Practice Administrators stopped at Connect calendar because they did not think they could approve Compass AI's access to patient appointments, and both IT Administrators asked which fields are read before connecting ( 2026-02-03-usability-test-compass-ai-practice-setup-flow/session-notes.md:51-52 ). This session asked one IT Administrator who approves access requests across practices what has to happen before a new system can read a practice's appointment schedule, and who can start that. It is about admin setup of Compass AI, not staff training (in 2026-02-10-ambient-scribe-medication-flag-concept/session-notes.md:62 , "onboarding" means training copy for clinicians).
> Method
> Method: 1:1 interview, 45 min
> Researcher: Priya Patel
> Full participant roster: see participants.md in this folder
> Context: The participant is an IT Administrator who handles access requests for systems used across Meridian's practices. They had not used the Compass AI practice setup flow. The researcher described steps 3 (Connect calendar) and 4 (Invite your team) as the 2026-02-03 build shows them, and what the step 3 screen does and doesn't say, without sharing what other participants said. The participant did not name the scheduling system that holds the appointment schedule, and these notes don't record which system it is. This is their account of the approval process, not a review of its records.
> Key Findings
> [HIGH] (schedule access: privacy review) In their account, any new system that reads the appointment schedule needs a privacy review request before it is connected, and a Practice Administrator can't start one: requests are filed by IT. **[3] Contextual Inquiry — Session Lock During Active Ambient Scribe Dictation** · `raw:2026-02-17-session-lock-during-dictation`

It also complicates the v2 measure: "clicked into at least one citation" records that a citation was opened, not what the nurse checked once it was.
> [MEDIUM] (manual cases, claim denials and calls) Cases outside the pilot's straightforward scope they do by hand from the start. For a request that follows a claim denial, they begin from the denial letter's stated reason and then gather the chart evidence that answers it; in their view a draft built from the chart would start in the wrong place, because the reason is in the letter, not the chart. Before a call with the health plan about a case, they prepare by hand too, keeping their own list of chart references to have open during the call. They described the calls as the hardest part of the week to plan for, but gave no time figure. For a straightforward case that the health plan later asks about, they said they find the evidence again from scratch; they didn't know whether a submitted request keeps the panel's citations.
