# Survey — Nursing Staff Attitudes Toward AI-Assisted Documentation

## Objective
Gauge baseline sentiment and specific concerns among nursing staff ahead of any AI documentation pilot, and identify which units are most receptive to early testing.

## Method
- **Method:** Online survey, distributed via internal staff portal, 12 questions (Likert + 2 open-ended)
- **Researcher:** Priya Patel
- **Full participant roster:** see `participants.md` in this folder

## Key Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** *(sentiment)* 61% of respondents were 'neutral to skeptical' about AI-generated documentation being accurate enough to sign off on without heavy editing; 24% were optimistic; 15% strongly opposed.
- **[MEDIUM]** *(concern)* The top volunteered concern (open-ended, coded from 89 responses) was 'losing my clinical narrative voice / it will sound robotic,' not accuracy or privacy as originally hypothesized.
- **[LOW]** *(unit variance)* ICU and ED nurses were notably more skeptical than Med-Surg and outpatient clinic nurses, likely correlated with documentation complexity/acuity.
- **[LOW]** *(openness to pilot)* 38% of respondents volunteered interest in being part of an early pilot group when asked directly.

## Representative Quotes
> "My notes are how I hand off a patient safely. If a robot writes them and I'm just rubber-stamping, that scares me more than typing."
> — Registered Nurse, ICU, P07 (survey open-ended)

> "Honestly if it got me out of the room 10 minutes faster to chart, I'd try almost anything."
> — Registered Nurse, Outpatient Clinic, P41 (survey open-ended)

## Recommendations
1. Recruit the initial usability testing panel from the 38% who volunteered, weighted toward Med-Surg/outpatient where skepticism is lower, to build early momentum before tackling ICU/ED.
2. Reframe internal AI messaging around 'voice preservation' and editability, not just time savings — this addresses the top volunteered concern directly.

## Follow-ups / Open Questions
- Cross-tab results by tenure — does skepticism correlate with years of experience?
- Consider a focused follow-up survey for ICU/ED specifically given their outsized skepticism.
